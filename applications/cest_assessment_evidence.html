3. Why Self-Employed: Factor Analysis

Self-Employment Indicators (Decisive)

HMRC determined self-employed because the client does not have control over this work. The following factors were decisive:

  • Sole discretion over manner and means (Cl. 1.2) — contractor decides how work is done
  • Control over time and place (Cl. 1.3) — contractor sets own hours and location
  • Autonomous collaboration (Preamble C, D) — contract explicitly excludes client direction/control
  • Task-specific engagement (Cl. 1.1) — scope changes require new written agreement
  • No corporate benefits (Cl. 3.1) — no pension, holiday, sick pay
  • No management authority (Cl. 3.2) — cannot bind client or manage employees
  • Independent status (Cl. 3.1, 3.3) — explicitly not a contract of employment
  • Substitution right exists (Cl. 1.4) — contractor may appoint substitutes (subject to client approval)

Employment Indicators (Present but Outweighed)

These factors point toward employment but were insufficient to change the overall determination:

  • Non-compete clause (Cl. 7.4) — restricts competitive work during contract term
  • IP assignment (Cl. 4.2) — all work product belongs to client
  • Client can reject substitutes (Cl. 1.4) — substitution not unconditional
  • Fixed monthly rate — time-based rather than project/deliverable-based pay
  • Majority of working time — primary income source
  • First contract, no prior self-employed history — no established pattern of contracting
  • Fix work at usual rate — no financial risk from unsatisfactory work
Note on "control" and code reviews: The CEST "control" test assesses whether the client dictates how work is performed (process control), not whether quality assurance practices exist. Code reviews, merge approvals, and CI checks are industry-standard quality gates applied equally to employees and contractors. Working within an existing tech stack (Python, TypeScript) and following existing architecture patterns are project specifications, not process control. HMRC's Employment Status Manual (ESM11070) distinguishes between controlling the process and checking deliverable quality.
