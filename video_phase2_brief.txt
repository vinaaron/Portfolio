AVIINI - THE MIDDLE + THE END (Jul 2023 - Jan 2025)
---------------------------------------------------
(Phase 1 covers the start. This is what happened during and how it ended.)

The breakthrough product: Pokémon keychains

Aaron's words:
"We followed the course and had a task to find out 30 products
for different niches and funnily enough that intro task was
about the only useful thing on the course because we found one
product on there which I was like to my mate Avi 'lets put this
on Etsy.' My mate was the one who was like we should try Etsy
long before we got the course actually. However, this product —
Pokémon keychains — was what began our journey to hit 6 figures
in revenue for e-commerce."

The day-to-day:
"As monotonous as you would imagine. Go on AliExpress, research
products, find them, put them onto each store, repeat. Get
orders, put in customer details, order directly to them, track
the revenue, repeat. Very mundane but that is the process to
scale simple businesses like that. To be honest all businesses
have their mundane got-to-be-done tasks."

Product pivots:
Pokémon keychains → home decor (ceiling lamps, table lamps) → clothing

How it ended:
"After pivoting from different products from home decor like
ceiling lamps and table lamps to clothing, we were hit with IP
related concerns because we were selling artist-related clothing.
It came to the point where we even got fake sued. That led us
to stop pursuing ecom."

The lesson:
"This was the best decision for us because it was like a slap
on the wrist from the man above. We didn't receive punishment
and we stopped this quick money, only monetary focused business
which had no tangential benefit to reality. We were purely
allowing consumerism, adding no value to the society we were
in. That is the lesson which is finally making more and more
sense to me — chase impact, chase value, chase virtue, not
useless monetary outcomes."


THOUGHTS2ACTIONS (Jan 2025)
---------------------------
The problem it solved:
"You know that feeling when you feel like you've forgotten
something — an idea, a problem, a potential solution. With
thoughts2actions that will no longer happen. When that new
idea pops in your head just quickly record it, let AI
transcribe and synthesize it for you to come back to later."

Context: Built in 7 days. Built during the "12 in 12" phase
(12 startups in 12 months).

Launch:
"I launched it on various platforms. My LinkedIn, which was
cool — first time sort of revealing my entrepreneurial nature
to my friends from school and uni."

What went wrong (his words):
"It was always this stunt for me. I was cloudy on the vision
and after I stopped the fun part — building it — I immediately
ran away from the product during the potential hard part of
distributing it. It was too rushed and I didn't really have
any understanding of the problem space and quit before I
even really started."


SIGN LANGUAGE DETECTION — HACKATHON (Feb 2025)
----------------------------------------------
Aaron frames this as "a side quest."

The problem it solved:
"A sign language detection model via Web3 technologies which
could be used to decentralised store new training data and
help improve the underlying model. Users could come to the
site, create ASL training data — it gets stored decentralised
and then can be used for retraining the model."

Result: Won $2,500

Honest caveats Aaron wants to share:
"I did have a slight advantage because my potential partner
was a part of the organisers, but she was too scared to fully
be a part of my team so she did drop out haha. I do think
majority of the work I would like to take credit for. TBH it
was mostly Cursor and GPT at the time but the ideation was me."

What it felt like:
"Winning the 2.5K was cool — it was validation that I am
capable of cool things. Apparently it is not common to win
your first hackathon."

The lesson:
"The lesson there is always try. At the beginning I went in
for the monetary target TBH, but I got to learn about cool
technologies like Filecoin, Akave, Storacha, Lilypad. Things
I would have never known about, and I got to showcase a
great thing that built up my experiences."


LINQ (Sep 2025 - Jan 2026)
--------------------------
Role: CTO

The problem it solved:
"An app where you can be matched with the ideal person to
network with. Then it became the place creators can network
with each other. Imagine a Tinder-esque swiping system for
networking — that was LINQ."

Why Aaron left:
"Unfortunately, the team and I were not aligned and that led
me to part ways. I felt I was not being fairly compensated
for my hard work via the equity because I was not getting
comp outside of that. I was putting in the most effort and
work and was compensated less."

The lesson (on co-founders):
"I have learnt a lot over the years about potential
co-founders and I think I will be asking a lot more about
them before I ever enter a new one because it's like a
marriage — it can be one of the most important people in
your life. Smart, honest and hard working — a tough combo.
I think the biggest lesson from that was co-founders and
making sure you understand all the nuances going into a big
relationship like that and who the people are. If they treat
contracts as king, think more carefully."

What LINQ did give him:
"I was able to do a ton of cloud deployment for LINQ and it
showed me how powerful Claude and these tools already are at
it, so it has demystified a lot of that realm for me which
is great."
