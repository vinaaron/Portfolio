# VIDEO #1 — BEAT SHEET (v2)

The honest journey video. Hook: 9 months / £32 revenue.
Spine: what entrepreneurship actually looks like, not what social media sells.
Mission anchor: ScrollBuddy = impact over revenue.


## HOW TO READ THIS

This is a BEAT SHEET, not a script. Two rules:

1. **BEATS** (numbered bullets) are anchors. You say each beat in your own
   words. The bullets constrain the claim so you don't ramble — they don't
   write the line for you.

2. **VERBATIM** sections (marked clearly) are the lines that have to land
   exactly. Hook. Contract. Mission. CTA. Lesson. About 6 lines across
   the whole video. Read these as written. Everything else is yours.

The **ARC** line at the top of each Part shows the emotional shape of
that Part. Read it before the take. If the take ends and the arc didn't
move, do another take.

The **AVOID** notes are the common slips that would betray the tone.
Glance at them before each beat.

## PART 1 — THE PROBLEM, IDEA AND HOW IT STARTED

**Setup:** Living room. Wider camera, NOT below eye level. Walking to
desk, setting up camera, opening laptop. Lock exposure first so the
light doesn't drift mid-take.

**Arc:** Punch the hook → frame what's coming → why you built this thing
specifically → state the mission → set the contract with the viewer →
bridge to Part 2.

**Total:** 8 beats, ~75 sec.

---

**BEAT 1.1 — The hook (VERBATIM)**

> **VERBATIM:** "Almost 9 months after constantly working on an idea,
> building the app, and marketing it — here's everything I have to show
> for it. Thirty-two pounds in revenue."

- Deliver deadpan. The vulnerability is the strength.
- *Avoid:* don't smile defensively. Don't tee it up with "so basically".
  Hit it cold.

---

**BEAT 1.2 — What this video is**

- Tell the viewer what the next 10 minutes covers: what happened, where
  things are now, the plan from here.
- Frame as the complete journey — idea, app, first 1,000 users, hopefully
  one day revolutionising doomscrolling.
- *Avoid:* don't oversell. The "revolutionising" word only works if you
  half-laugh at the ambition while saying it.

---

**BEAT 1.3 — Channel context**

- Three years of working on apps and businesses on this channel.
- This time, building something that solves your own problem.

---

**BEAT 1.4 — Your own problem**

- You were doomscrolling. Hours a day.
- The "hated it, knew it, couldn't stop" confession — this is the WHY.
- *Avoid:* don't generalise to "we all do it". Make it specific to you.

---

**BEAT 1.5 — ScrollBuddy was born**

- 8 months ago. Built to give people back the time their phone is stealing.
- One sentence — the simplest version of what ScrollBuddy is.

---

**BEAT 1.6 — The mission (VERBATIM)**

> **VERBATIM:** "Impact, not revenue. If ScrollBuddy gives one person
> back an hour a day, that matters more than the MRR ever will."

- Don't rush this one. Each half-sentence is its own beat.
- *Avoid:* don't qualify it. Don't say "I mean, obviously I'd like
  revenue too". The line works because it doesn't hedge.

---

**BEAT 1.7 — The contract (VERBATIM)**

> **VERBATIM:** "Before I go on — this isn't a highlight reel. You're
> going to see the real numbers, the real doubts, and the part where
> I almost quit. If you want polished entrepreneurship content, you're
> in the wrong place."

- Level eye contact through the whole line.
- *Avoid:* don't smile at the end. Don't laugh through it. This is the
  channel's deal with the viewer.

---

**BEAT 1.8 — Bridge to Part 2**

- One sentence: "And so I went on building ScrollBuddy."
- The simplest version. Don't elaborate. Don't preview Part 2.

---

**BEAT 2.1 — Joining LINQ**

- You joined LINQ as CTO. Platform partnered with Simon Squibb.
- Set this up factually. Don't over-explain Simon or LINQ — Kharel's
  b-roll will fill context.

[Insert here: Kharel's b-roll about LINQ — the office, the platform,
the partnership context.]

**BEAT 2.2 — Two apps at once (the confession)**

- ScrollBuddy + LINQ in parallel.
- Name the cost cleanly: neither got your full attention.

## PART 3 — GRINDING & STAYING GROUNDED

**Setup:** Continue at desk for most of Part 3. The walk only happens at
the very end (BEAT 3.8). This protects you from the "nowhere to look
while walking" problem Kharel flagged in the original.

**Arc:** Doubt → sit with it → see what you'd been doing wrong (the
turn) → decision → action → payoff → honest about the cost → balance
reset → walk out to badminton.

**Total:** 8 beats, ~75 sec.

**What got relocated out of this Part since v1:**
- Short-form posting / goals / results → moved to Part 4 (where it fits
  the 1,030-users story naturally).
- Bug-fixing / improving app simultaneously → moved to Part 5 (where it
  fits the "jumping on user calls" plan).

---

**BEAT 3.1 — The crash after LINQ**

- The day or two post-LINQ where you didn't know what to do.
- Name the doubt out loud: "maybe entrepreneurship isn't for me."
- *Avoid:* don't pile on a second doubt phrase. Pick the one and let it
  sit.

---

**BEAT 3.2 — The realisation (the turn)**

- Once you sat with the doubt a couple days, you saw something.
- For 3 years you'd been throwing darts at a wall — building solutions,
  then trying to find problems to match.
- The metaphor IS the point. Say it clean.
- *Avoid:* don't apologise for the 3 wasted years. Naming the mistake
  is the strength of the beat.

---

**BEAT 3.3 — The decision**

- Once you saw the pattern, the move was obvious: do the opposite.
- Stop inventing the next thing. Go find people who were already
  building something real.

---

**BEAT 3.4 — The action**

- Cold emails. Applications. Constant.
- About a month of it.
- Landing: Cogram.

---

**BEAT 3.5 — The payoff**

- What landing Cogram actually changed: income, stability, brain space
  to keep ScrollBuddy alive on the side.
- *Avoid:* don't perform gratitude. Don't say "I'm so lucky". Just
  state the change.

---

**BEAT 3.6 — The honest caveat**

- But the focus was split. And it stayed split.
- This is the beat that earns the viewer's trust — by not lying about
  the cost.

---

**BEAT 3.7 — Family / balance**

- Working two things is a lot. But friends and family aren't the trade-off.
- Balance matters.

---

**BEAT 3.8 — Walk out (VERBATIM punchline)**

- Stand up, close laptop, pick up bag.

> **VERBATIM:** "So on that note... time to be a bit social."

- Hand covers camera as you say "social" → cut.

[Cut to badminton b-roll / montage.]

## PART 4 — THE REAL NUMBERS

**Setup:** Return from badminton. Holding osmo (handheld), walking back
to your room. Settle, place the camera on the desk, open laptop while
talking. Slightly more centred than Part 1.

**Arc:** Numbers cold → honesty reminder → critique the highlight-reel
industry → reveal how the users actually came (short-form grind) → the
reframe (£30 isn't failure) → mission callback → impact-as-the-real-number.

**Total:** 10 beats, ~100 sec.

---

**BEAT 4.1 — Transition into numbers**

- Until now you've talked about how it started. Time to talk about how
  it's doing.
- Frame as "the real numbers".

---

**BEAT 4.2 — Drop the numbers cold**

- Eight months in.
- 1,030 users.
- 400 active every month.
- £30 in recurring revenue.
- Hit each number distinctly. Don't run them together.
- *Avoid:* don't soften them. Don't say "only" or "just". The numbers
  are the numbers.

---

**BEAT 4.3 — Honesty reminder (echo of the contract)**

- Going to be completely honest about all of this.
- Sugarcoating defeats the whole point of this channel.

---

**BEAT 4.4 — The industry critique**

- Startups get glorified online.
- Founders post the wins and skip the long painful stretches.
- *Avoid:* don't name specific creators. Don't be petty.

---

**BEAT 4.5 — The real texture**

- It's not what it looks like in the highlight reels.
- Demanding. Risky. Most days quietly brutal.
- One-word landings work here — let each word breathe.

---

**BEAT 4.6 — How the users actually came (relocated content)**

- Most of those 1,030 users came from short-form.
- You, filming, posting — often awkward, often ignored — for months.

---

**BEAT 4.7 — The first few videos got nothing**

- Be specific about how flat the start was.
- The reason you kept going: the alternative was no users at all.

---

**BEAT 4.8 — The reframe (THE TURN of Part 4)**

- £30 a month isn't failure.
- It's just not the metric you're measuring.
- This is the beat the whole video pivots on. Land it clean.

---

**BEAT 4.9 — Mission callback**

- ScrollBuddy is free.
- The only paid thing is the AI partner — and that's because it actually
  costs you to run.
- Everything else is free, because impact is the point, not the price.

---

**BEAT 4.10 — The real number**

- A thousand people using something you built to give them their
  attention back — that's the number you care about.
- Revenue follows or it doesn't, but the impact is real.

## PART 5 — THE PLAN

**Setup:** Same desk shot as end of Part 4. Slight forward energy — this
is the "what's next" beat.

**Arc:** This is just the beginning → name your weak link (consistency)
→ what you're doing on ScrollBuddy now → the CTA → more is coming.

**Total:** 5 beats, ~50 sec.

---

**BEAT 5.1 — Setup**

- This is just the beginning of a long journey.
- One sentence. Declaration.

---

**BEAT 5.2 — Your weak link**

- Biggest challenge has always been consistency. Showing up when
  nobody's watching.
- Doubling down on that now.
- *Avoid:* don't list excuses for past inconsistency. Just own it.

---

**BEAT 5.3 — What you're doing on ScrollBuddy (relocated content)**

- Jumping on calls with existing users.
- Taking their feedback.
- Fixing the bugs they hit.
- Working on what would make ScrollBuddy genuinely better.

---

**BEAT 5.4 — The CTA (VERBATIM)**

> **VERBATIM:** "If you want to be part of that — download ScrollBuddy
> from the App Store. Try it. Tell me what's broken. Tell me what would
> help."

- *Avoid:* don't soften the ask. Don't say "no pressure". Don't say
  "if you can". State it.

---

**BEAT 5.5 — Forward**

- ScrollBuddy isn't the only thing you'll be building.
- More is coming. Some will work. Most won't.
- The viewer sees all of it.

## PART 6 — OUTRO

**Setup:** Walking outside ideally — matches the "raw real journey" tone.
Handheld or osmo. If filming outside isn't possible, the desk works as
fallback but loses texture.

**Arc:** Acknowledge this video looks different → Kharel reveal (full
circle) → channel's thesis → contrast with content gurus → the lesson
lands → subscribe close.

**Total:** 6 beats, ~60 sec.

---

**BEAT 6.1 — This looks different**

- If they've seen older videos, this one's intentionally different.

---

**BEAT 6.2 — The Kharel reveal**

- Upgraded gear. Brought on Kharel as editor + social media manager.
- The full-circle bit: he was actually one of your early viewers on
  the live streams.
- *Avoid:* don't oversell Kharel. The full-circle fact speaks for itself.

---

**BEAT 6.3 — The channel's thesis**

- Why this setup: you want to be an entrepreneur who shows the journey
  by doing it.
- Not a content person who does things for the content.

---

**BEAT 6.4 — The contrast (gentle, not bitter)**

- Most "gurus" online are content creators first.
- They do things for the content.
- You're trying to do the opposite — do the thing, let people watch.
- *Avoid:* don't name names. Don't get bitter. The contrast is the
  point, not the takedown.

---

**BEAT 6.5 — The lesson lands (VERBATIM — uses verb A by default)**

> **VERBATIM:** "If you take one thing from this video — define your
> win before you pick the metric. Mine isn't pounds in the bank. It's
> people getting their hours back."

**If you swap to verb B or C, replace BEAT 6.5 with:**

> **[B]:** "If you take one thing from this video — show up unglamorous.
> The version of you that's not optimised for the camera is the one
> that builds something real."

> **[C]:** "If you take one thing from this video — keep showing up.
> The metrics will lie to you about whether it's working. Trust the
> work instead."

---

**BEAT 6.6 — The close**

- Lots more coming from this channel.
- Make sure they're subscribed.
- "I'll see you in the next one."
- *Avoid:* don't beg for the subscribe. State it.

## DELIVERY NOTES (for Aaron)

- Read the **Arc** line of a Part before starting any of its takes. If
  the take ends and the arc didn't move, do another take.
- Read the bullets of a beat 30 seconds before the take. Don't memorise
  them. Just internalise the claim, then say it your way.
- Verbatim lines are the only memorisation. Six total across the whole
  video.
- If you catch yourself rambling mid-take, stop. Reset. The bullet exists
  to constrain the claim — if you're not on the claim anymore, you're
  off the beat.
- The "Avoid" notes are the failure modes. Glance at them once before
  each beat. They're not rules; they're flags for the slips that would
  cheapen the tone.
